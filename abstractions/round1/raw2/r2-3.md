## 1. Two-Reader Coherence: L3 restated over the collector's own classifier

**Seed driver:** `GC` at the tail, and `YK·O·YK`.

**Relation to round 1: an objection with a counterexample.** Every round-1 C3 idea states relocation over the VM sort of a word:
- R1-1 #2 Relocation Equivariance
- R1-2 #2 layout DSL ("the VM value's constructor decides whether the word is a pointer")
- R1-3 #2 E1 ("no raw word is ever mis-rewritten")
- R1-4 #2 ("int/code/atom/headers are fixed because non-pointer words are copied")
- R1-5 #2 Pseudomorph
- R1-6 #2 Aureola ("ruled out by `Word'` typing, which requires the layout to carry the scan/no-scan tag")

The round-1 fact sheet now confirms that the collector classifies words by bit pattern: an even word in the young range is treated as a pointer.

The R1-6 #2 escape does not work. The scan/no-scan tag is per block (`tag ≥ No_scan_tag`), not per word. Inside a scannable block, and on the VM stack (a root region), every word is classified by its bits. So a `Val.raw w` with `w` even and inside the young range gets rewritten by the machine GC but not by `ρ♯`.

The toy file proves this with `decide`:
- placement: block 0 at `0x1008`, young range `[0x1000, 0x2000)`, moved to `0x9000`
- a raw `0x1008` in a stack slot
- result: `¬ H5stated … (.raw 0x1008)`

So H5 as posed is false. Under Law 4 this is the obstruction, not a workaround.

R1-6 #5 Keratectomy (`peel`) decodes by bits. That matches the collector, but then `peel` disagrees with the VM state on exactly the same words. The premise is needed on that side too.

**A second defect, from memory [check against the 4.14 `minor_gc.c`].** In `oldify_one` the collector reads the header at `v − 8` (`Hd_val`). The only interior pointers it handles are `Infix_tag` offsets inside closure blocks.
- So `ptr ℓ k` with `k > 0` is relocated correctly only when word `k−1` of block ℓ is an Infix header.
- R1-2 #2's R-Translate (`ρ(πℓ+8k) = ρ(πℓ)+8k` for all `k ≤ size`) and R1-4 #2's block-affinity build in an assumption that does not hold.
- L3's 20,000-heap check probably used the law's own relocation model rather than the collector [check how the census generated its heaps].

**The idea.** Keep any round-1 C3 framework, but define the relocation action by the collector's classifier. Then prove one reconciliation rule saying that the two readers agree on coherent values. Coherence becomes a named field of the loop-head relation, preserved by the steps.

**Core rules:**
- **K0 (classifier).** `cls Y w := if w odd then imm else if w ∈ Y then young else opaque`. `Y` is the minor-heap range, fixed for the run unless `Gc.set` resizes it [check].
- **K1 (collector law, L3 at the machine).** On scanned cells, the GC maps `w ↦ fwd w` when `cls Y w = young`, and copies the word otherwise. This is proved once against the collector, over bits.
- **K2 (coherence atom).** `Coh Y π v` is a named-field structure:
  - `raw w`: `w` is odd or `w ∉ Y`.
  - `ptr ℓ k`: either `k = 0`, or `InfixAt ℓ k`.
  - `code` / `atom`: their words lie outside `Y` (text and static data).
  - `int`: always true.
- **K3 (reconciliation, the rule that makes L3 true).** `Coh Y π v → gcWord (enc π v) = enc (r∘π) v`. L3 becomes K1 composed with K3, so every round-1 equivariance proof goes through with one side atom added.
- **K4 (stability).** `Coh Y π v → Coh Y (r∘π) v`. This holds because `r` maps young blocks into the major heap and `Y` is fixed.
- **K5 (preservation, where L4 is used).** Only instructions that write a raw word into a scanned cell carry a Coh obligation. The list of raw producers is static [check which `BcSem` primitives produce `raw`]. Raws inside no-scan blocks (strings, doubles, custom blocks) need nothing, because K1 copies them.

**What it makes free, and what it costs.**
- *Free:* the round-1 C3 machinery becomes sound unchanged, apart from the added atom.
- *Free:* the gap round 1 flagged but left open becomes one typed, named premise.
- *Cost:* Infix handling for closures.
- *Cost:* the raw-producer census.
- *Cost:* Coh is added to the loop-head relation, as one of its 15 fields plus one.

**Distant theories:**
- Signal aliasing (the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem). An undersampled signal cannot be told apart from its alias. Coherence is the anti-aliasing condition.
- Conservative and mostly-copying collection. Bartlett (1988) [check] pins pages reached by ambiguous roots. Boehm–Weiser (1988) [check].
- Linguistic homographs: the same spelling carries two meanings, and the sort acts as the disambiguating context.
- Coherence of two abstraction maps (Galois connections that agree on a sublattice).

**Clusters:** C3 (primary) and C4 (K5).

**Pilot:**
- **H5.**
  - As stated, it is refuted (the toy file's `decide`).
  - Repaired, it is `h5_coh`: `Coh v → (∀ l k, v = ptr l k → k = 0) → gcWord (enc pl v) = enc (fwd∘pl) v`. This is proved in the toy file in 7 lines by `cases v` and `omega`.
  - In the project, add the Infix case, which should be about 15 lines.
- **H6.** Split by header tag.
  - No-scan objects: K1 copies every word, so no Coh is needed.
  - Scan objects: `∀ i, Coh (field i)` plus K3, mapped over the fields.
- **H7.** The stack is a root region, so every slot is scanned. The premise is `∀ i, Coh (stk i)`, and the proof is K3 mapped over the slots.
- **H8/H9.** These only use `int` values, which are always coherent (they are odd). K5 is vacuous.
- **H1–H4 and the three refactors:** not applicable, because this idea is about C3 only.

## 2. Scan-Set Relocation: the collector reaches only roots plus the remembered set

**Seed driver:** `RR` (remembered references) and `cc66` (card pairs).

**Relation to round 1: an objection.**
- R1-6 #2 claims that "old-to-young pointers are handled for free, because `reloc` rewrites globally".
- R1-2 #5 R-GC-spec ("`ρ♯` on the young region, identity elsewhere"), R1-5 #2 R-GC-is-ρ, R1-3 #5 G3 and R1-1 #2 E4 all assume the collector's effect is a global pushforward.

A minor collector does not scan the major heap. It rewrites an old→young pointer only if that pointer's address is recorded in the remembered set (`Caml_state->ref_table` in 4.14 [check]). The write barrier (`caml_modify`) records it; young→young initialisation (`Alloc_small`) is not recorded.

Without an invariant, an unlogged old field that points into the young range becomes dangling. L3 in its global form is therefore a consequence of a representation field that round 1 never named.

**The idea.** Specify the GC as `ρ♯` restricted to a scan set:
`S(m) = roots (VM stack region, saved accu/env, globals, local roots) ∪ ref_table entries ∪ fields of copied blocks`.

Then add a named `RemSet` field to the representation. This splits C3 into three parts:
- the collector computes the scan-restricted pushforward (proved once, at the machine);
- `RemSet` makes the restricted and global pushforwards agree on the rendered footprint (one lemma);
- field writes preserve `RemSet` (the write-barrier obligation, attached to exactly one operation; see idea 4).

**Core rules:**
- **S1 (collector law).** `gc m = ρ♯` on `S(m)`, copy elsewhere, with forwarding headers left in from-space. From-space is garbage afterwards, so no represented predicate may mention it.
- **S2 (coverage, which yields L3 as a derived rule).** `RemSet m Y T := ∀ a ∈ major, cls Y (m a) = young → a ∈ T`. From it: `RemSet → ∀ a ∈ dom (render π s), a ∈ S(m) ∨ cls Y (m a) ≠ young`. Hence `ρ♯|S = ρ♯` on the footprint, and the round-1 equivariance theorems apply.
- **S3 (barrier rule, where L4 is used).** A write `m[a := w]` preserves `RemSet` iff one of these holds:
  - `a ∈ Y`;
  - `cls Y w ≠ young`;
  - `a` is appended to `T`.
- **S4 (reset).** After a minor GC, no live block lies in `Y`, so `RemSet` holds with `T = ∅`. This is why `RemSet` never has to be tracked across collections.

**What it makes free, and what it costs.**
- *Free:* the collector obligation becomes honest and local, and every old/young interaction is funnelled through S3.
- *Cost:* the collector proof over the scan set, which is still the hard one.
- *Cost:* the S3 obligations in the Layer-A arms for `SETFIELD`, `SETVECTITEM`, `SETFLOATFIELD` and the `caml_modify` call sites.

**Distant theories:**
- Incremental view maintenance over delta logs (Gupta–Mumick [check]). The ref table is the change log, and the GC is a view refresh that visits only logged tuples.
- Gauss's divergence theorem and boundary flux. What crosses the old|young boundary is determined by measurement at the boundary, and the remembered set is that boundary register.
- Chandy–Lamport snapshots (recording in-transit channel state), used loosely: old→young pointers are the in-flight messages.
- Generation scavenging (Ungar 1984 [check]) and card marking (Hölzle 1993 [check]).

**Clusters:** C3 (primary) and C4 (S3 at write sites).

**Pilot:**
- **H5 and H7.** Values and stack slots are roots, which are always in `S`. So S2 is trivial, and the proof goes through idea 1's K3.
- **H6, split in two:**
  - a young object: its fields are scanned when the object is copied;
  - an old object with a young field: needs `a ∈ T` from `RemSet`.
- **Obstruction to state (not checked here):** a two-block memory with an unlogged old→young field. On it, `gc m ≠ global ρ♯ m`, decidable on concrete words.
- **H8/H9.** No heap writes, so S3 is vacuous.
- **H1–H4 and the refactors:** not applicable.

## 3. Outcome Institutions: one orbit, many outcome signatures, lossy transport, and the H3 obstruction

**Seed driver:** `H3`, and the three palindromes `ZRZ`, `zHz`, `ZWZ`: a fixed frame around a varying outcome centre.

**Relation to round 1: extends and objects.**

It extends the round-1 orbit convergence (R1-3 #1 absorbing ℕ-orbits, R1-5 #1 Decay Chain, R1-6 #1 Capot) and their transport rules (R1-4 #1 R6, R1-5 #1 R-Transport, R1-6 #1 R-transport).

It objects in three places:
- **(a) Mathlib is not available.** R1-2 #1 and R1-4 #1 rest on Mathlib `Computation`, and R1-4 #5 on Mathlib `KleeneAlgebra`/`gfp`. The project has no Mathlib, so those must be discarded or rebuilt; the fuel-iterate variants survive. Whether `Relation.ReflTransGen` exists in core or Batteries is unverified [check]. If it is missing, define `Reach` inductively once.
- **(b) The transport is lossy.** `BcModel` instantiates `MachineModel` with `StepResult = next | halt | stuck`, so the outcome map `g` collapses `Wrong` and `Unsupported` into `stuck`. Round-1 transport treats `g` as harmless. It is not: "never Wrong" cannot be expressed on the MachineModel side, so H3 cannot be derived from H4 through `BcModel`.
- **(c) H3 as stated is false.** A never-Wrong program whose first instruction is Unsupported neither halts nor diverges. The witness is a one-instruction program with an opcode or primitive outside the fragment, refuted by `decide +kernel` on the concrete run. Round 1 flagged this as a caveat. I propose a static fix rather than a dynamic premise.

**Core rules (Lean core only):**
- **O-Iter (L1 as the definition).** `iter f : ℕ → Next C R → Next C R`, with `stop` absorbing. It is written in bind-at-front form so that `decide +kernel` reduces it. The n-step relation is `iter n (go c) = go c'`.
- **O-Act / O-Absorb / O-Unique / O-Tri.** As in round 1, proved once for the class.
- **O-Sig (outcome signature).** `R` comes with `kind : R → {halt, bad}`. A morphism `(h : C → C', g : R → R')` must satisfy `step' ∘ h = Next.map h g ∘ step` and must be halt-reflecting: `kind' (g r) = halt ↔ kind r = halt`.
- **O-Sat (satisfaction condition).** Let φ be an outcome formula over the target signature Σ′: halts-with, diverges, or stops-bad. Then `c ⊨ g*φ ↔ h c ⊨ φ`. Divergence and halting transport in both directions. Formulas that distinguish kinds collapsed by `g` do not transport; this is a proved non-rule, stated by the witness above.
- **O-Static (effect certificate).** `Supported P : Bool` scans the code's opcodes and primitives against the fragment ledger. `Supported P = true → ∀ n s, iter n s ≠ stop unsupported`. It is proved once, by sweeping the `Unsupported` arms. If some `Unsupported` is data-dependent [check], the certificate carries a residue list of dynamic sites.

**What it makes free, and what it costs.**
- *Free:* C1 once.
- *Free:* every future step relation with a lossy view pays one morphism square.
- *Free:* H3-style liveness has a decidable static premise.
- *Cost:* about 15 `iter` simp lemmas (there is no `Function.iterate_add` in core).
- *Cost:* the `Unsupported`-arm sweep.

**Distant theories:**
- Institution theory (Goguen–Burstall 1992 [check]): the satisfaction condition along signature morphisms.
- Coarse-graining and renormalisation in physics: which observables survive the coarse-graining map.
- Effect systems (Lucassen–Gifford 1988 [check]) for `Supported`.
- Progress in type soundness (Wright–Felleisen 1994 [check]): `Supported` plays the role of a progress premise.

**Clusters:** C1 (primary) and C2 (`Supported` is a checker whose soundness is O-Static).

**Pilot:**
- **H1:** `stop_unique`, from O-Absorb and `Nat.le_total`, about 3 lines.
- **H2:** `reach_iff_exists_iter`, one generic induction.
- **H3:**
  - The obstruction is stated as a separate theorem.
  - The repaired statement is `(∀ n, iter n s ≠ stop wrong) → Supported P = true → (diverges s ↔ ¬ halts s)`, about 4 lines.
- **H4:** the same generic lemma on the `MachineModel` instance.
- **H8:** closed `decide +kernel` on a concrete state only. The universally quantified form is idea 4's job.
- **H5–H7, H9:** not applicable.
- **Refactor, VM run-determinism:** becomes `DetSys (BcSem.step P)` plus O-Unique.
- **Refactor, machine run-composition:** becomes `iter_add`.
- **Refactor, generic reachability ↔ VM n-step:** O-Sat with `h = id` and `g` the collapsing outcome map. `BcModel` is `BcSem.step` behind `g`, so the square closes by `cases`, about 5 lines.

## 4. Effect-Signature Step: the step as a free-monad program, with locality proved once as read-set validation

**Seed driver:** `Tokpet` (a token trace) and the `A`-alphabet `AB3`, `Ap`, `AhFB`, `Ai`.

**Relation to round 1: an objection and a replacement.** Six round-1 ideas prove L4 by a roughly 150-arm factorisation of `step` against a second, hand-written description of each instruction:
- R1-1 #3 Footprint Lenses
- R1-2 #3 Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon lenses
- R1-3 #3 Step patches
- R1-4 #3 Instruction patches
- R1-5 #3 Stencil
- R1-6 #3 Vividiffusion

Three concrete problems:
1. **The descriptions duplicate `stepI`** (Law 3).
2. **Data-dependent footprints need special handling.** `GETFIELD` through the accumulator and `APPTERM n` force dependent or two-phase footprints, which R1-2 #3 and R1-5 #3 already concede. Field lenses on a `List` heap with a symbolic ℓ also leave `ℓ ≠ ℓ'` side goals.
3. **Their H8 pilots (`rfl`, `intro σ E; rfl`) assume kernel defeq reduction with free variables.** `decide +kernel`, the project's tool, cannot close a goal with free variables, and `rfl` stalls on any well-founded recursion inside `step`.

**The idea.** Write each instruction once as a program over a small first-order effect signature. To stay in `Type` rather than bumping to `Type 1`, give every operation a fixed response type (no existential β):

`inductive VM α | ret a | rd (c : Cell) (k : Val → VM α) | wr (c : Cell) (v : Val) (k) | push v k | pop (k : Val → VM α) | alloc tag fs (k : Loc → VM α) | fail (e : Bad)`

Cells are `pc`, `accu`, `env`, `extra`, `trap`, `slot i`, `field ℓ i`, the world parts and `heapLen`. Then set `BcSem.step P s = runSt (stepF P (decode P s.pc)) s`.
- Either define `BcSem.step` this way, with the `runbc` difftest guarding it,
- or prove the equation once by `cases op <;> rfl`.

**Core rules:**
- **E1 (locality, L4 as a theorem proved by induction on `VM`: one case per operation constructor, not per opcode).** Take a partial handler `runP` over partial states: an `Option` per cell, and a known stack prefix over an unknown tail. Then `runP p m = some (a, p') → p ⊑ s → runSt m s = some (a, p' ▷ s)`, where `▷` overlays the written cells and keeps the tail.
  - Proved in the toy file (`locality`, with an auxiliary monotonicity lemma): about 40 lines, one case per constructor.
- **E2 (OCC validation).** The operations a run actually issued are its dynamic footprint. Data-dependent footprints cost nothing extra, because `runP` just follows the trace.
- **E3 (closed computation, then lift to all states).** A ∀-state spec is proved by one closed `decide +kernel` on `runP` from a mostly-unknown partial state, followed by E1.
- **E4 (escape).** When a run reads an unknown cell, `runP` returns `none`. Then either case-split that cell over a finite set, or fall back to R1-1 #5's value-generic stepping on that cell only.
- **E5 (fresh locations).** `alloc` in `runP` returns `fresh k`, and the overlay maps `fresh k ↦ s.heap.length + k`. This is the only symbolic term the handler needs.

**What it makes free, and what it costs.**
- *Free:* L4, once, with no lens library.
- *Free:* straight-line specs from any state become closed kernel computations.
- *Free:* `runP` is an executable checker whose soundness is E1 (C2).
- *Free:* a single place, `wr (field …)`, to attach idea 2's S3.
- *Cost:* transliterating `stepI` into `VM`, about 150 mechanical arms that `runbc` guards.
- *Cost:* kernel reduction through continuations (closures are fine in the kernel; the risk is performance [check at scale]).

**Distant theories:**
- Algebraic effects and handlers (Plotkin–Power 2002; Plotkin–Pretnar 2009 [check]); interaction trees (Xia et al., POPL 2020 [check]).
- Optimistic concurrency control (Kung–Robinson 1981 [check]): a transaction commits if its read set validates. `p ⊑ s` is exactly that validation step.
- Shadow-memory dynamic analysis (Valgrind, Nethercote–Seward 2007 [check]).
- Physics: a measurement record fixes only the observed quantities.

**Clusters:** C4 (primary) and C2. It feeds C3 through S3.

**Pilot:**
- **H8.** In the toy file, `h8_closed : runP h8prog ⟨none, []⟩ = some ((), ⟨some 42, []⟩) := by decide` and `h8_any (s) : run h8prog s = some ((), {s with accu := 42})`. The latter is 2 lines from `locality`, and the stack is unchanged by construction.
  - In the project: `∀ s, s.pc = 0 → iter 4 s = go {s with pc := 4, accu := int 42}`, using one `decide +kernel` plus E1.
- **H9.** The counter is an unknown cell, so E4 applies. Either:
  - 11 closed `decide`s over `Fin 11` starts, or
  - one body run with `i` symbolic plus a rank argument (idea 5 or idea 3's iterate).
- **H1–H7:** not applicable.
- **Refactors:** the determinism, composition and conversion blocks are untouched. `stepF` is a presentation of `step`, not a new relation.

## 5. VM Resource Algebra with operation-level small axioms: framing through the existing Iris WP

**Seed driver:** `H8` inside `cc66H8RR`, the palindrome `9R9` (a resource held between two fixed ends), and the bookends `6Vw6` / `IbX6…Ci6` (the ends indexed from the bottom).

**Relation to round 1: objects to a second program logic, and extends idea 4.**

The brief already has a generic Iris WP calculus with proved adequacy for any machine model, and `BcModel` instantiates it with `BcSem.step`. Four round-1 ideas each add a parallel C4 logic with its own soundness proof of about 200 lines:
- R1-2 #4 reachability logic
- R1-6 #4 Cumal ledger
- R1-1 #4 GKAT
- R1-4 #4 Poincaré

That is Law 3 duplication.

The lens ideas also index stack slots from the top (R1-3 #3 P2, R1-5 #3 `slot i`), which makes framing a stack tail require re-indexing on every push and pop. The machine addresses the stack through `sp`, so absolute depth from the bottom keeps ownership of the tail stable.

**The idea.** Give `BcModel` a ghost state interpretation:
- authoritative copies of `pc`, `accu`, `env`, `extra` and `trap`;
- the stack as a map from bottom-depth to `Val`, plus a height;
- the heap as a map from `Loc` to `Obj`, plus a monotone length (a max-nat fragment);
- the world.

The fragments are `pc ↦ p`, `acc ↦ v`, `stk[d] ↦ v`, `height ↦ h`, `ℓ.i ↦ v` and `heapLen ≥ n`.
- Prove one small axiom per operation of idea 4's signature, about 8 in total.
- Per-opcode WP rules are then derived by running `wp` over the `stepF` programs (`wp_bind` plus the axioms, by `simp`). They are never stated by hand.
- Framing is Iris's own `wp_frame`.

**Core rules:**
- **B1 (small axioms).** For example:
  - `acc ↦ v -∗ WP rd accu {w. ⌜w = v⌝ ∗ acc ↦ v}`
  - `height ↦ h ∗ stk[h−1] ↦ v -∗ WP pop {w. ⌜w = v⌝ ∗ height ↦ h−1}`
  - `heapLen ≥ n -∗ WP alloc … {ℓ. ⌜ℓ ≥ n⌝ ∗ ℓ.* ↦ fs ∗ heapLen ≥ ℓ+1}`
- **B2 (frame, L4 as a rule).** L4 is B1 plus Iris's frame rule. Old heap blocks and the stack below `h` are framed by ownership, with no `ℓ ≠ ℓ'` side goals.
- **B3 (derivation).** `wp (stepF P i) Φ` unfolds mechanically, so a per-instruction rule is a computation.
- **B4 (ranked loops).** Total correctness by Nat induction on the rank, outside Iris (total WP has no Löb). Partial correctness by Löb [check iris-lean's `later`/Löb API].
- **B5 (adequacy).** The existing adequacy theorem, which consumes idea 3's orbit for H1–H4-shaped conclusions.
- **Speculative C3 link:** the machine-side representation as ownership, with relocation as a frame-preserving ghost update.

**What it makes free, and what it costs.**
- *Free:* one program logic. Call-site reuse and framing are Iris's.
- *Free:* about 8 axioms instead of about 150 opcode rules.
- *Cost:* the state-interpretation proof for `BcModel` (about 300 lines [estimate]).
- *Cost:* iris-lean's maturity for authoritative maps and heap views [check].
- *Cost, honest:* WP does not count steps. So H8's "four steps" comes from idea 4. B5 gives "runs to STOP with `accu = 42` and the stack unchanged".

**Distant theories:**
- Separation logic's small axioms (O'Hearn–Reynolds–Yang 2001 [check]); Iris ghost state (Jung et al., JFP 2018 [check]); effect handlers in Iris (Hazel, de Vilhena–Pottier, POPL 2021 [check]).
- Double-entry bookkeeping: the authoritative ledger ● against the vouchers ◯.
- Hardware addressing: frame-pointer versus `sp`-relative addressing. Bottom-absolute depth is the frame-pointer choice.

**Clusters:** C4 (primary). C1 is reached only through adequacy.

**Pilot:**
- **H8:** `pc ↦ 0 ∗ acc ↦ a ∗ height ↦ h ⊢ WP {pc ↦ 4 ∗ acc ↦ int 42 ∗ height ↦ h}`. The slots below `h` are never mentioned, so they are framed. About 6 lines through the derived rules. The exact step count comes from idea 4.
- **H9:**
  - Invariant: `pc ↦ L ∗ height ↦ h ∗ stk[h−1] ↦ int i ∗ ⌜i ≤ 10⌝`.
  - Rank: `10 − i`, by B4.
  - The body comes from B3 with `i` symbolic.
  - About 15 lines.
- **H1–H4:** delegated to idea 3.
- **H5–H7:** not applicable.
- **Refactor, generic reachability ↔ VM n-step conversion:** that is the existing adequacy theorem, so this idea adds nothing to it.
